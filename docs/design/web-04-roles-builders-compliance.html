The front desk
does not hold a key.

Your instinct about the scheduler was right, and it is already written down — docs/COMPANION_ACCESS_CONTROL.md has the Front desk role, the three-plane rule, and an "asked and answered" row quoting almost your exact question. What it has never had is a screen. This deck renders that model, plus the plan builder, the tool builder, what a prescriber's account changes, and the honest answer on compliance tooling.

The modelAlready specified

Three planes, and who is allowed to stand in each.

Least privilege usually fails because it is enforced by hiding columns. Here it is enforced by key custody: a role decides what you may ask for, and only a patient's grant hands over a wrapped key. The front desk does not have a mood column switched off — they were never given anything that decrypts to a mood.

Data plane

Ciphertext, and the keys to it

Patients hold keys. Clinicians hold grants — a wrapped key the patient authorised. The server never sees plaintext.

Normally holds a key
Patient Clinician Psychiatrist Assistant · narrowed Supervisor · by grant only
Never
Front desk Org admin Sysadmin

A supervisor reads through an explicit, consented grant — never by title. Seniority is not a decryption key.

Control plane

Who may do what

Roles, membership, grant and revoke, step-up policy. Server-enforced, operating on the capability graph and metadata.

Operates here
Org admin Front desk · invites Patient · their own grants
Scope
One practice — not global

An org admin manages membership for their practice and can revoke anyone. Management authority and read capability are deliberately different objects.

Monitoring plane

What happened, not what was said

The hash-chained audit log and the behavioural guard. Metadata only, short retention, minimum logging.

Reads here
Patient · their own trail Org admin · review Sysadmin · ops only
Guard response
Step up, never hard-lock

A hard lockout could cut off a clinician mid-session with someone in crisis. The guard pauses a token and demands re-auth instead.

The rule that makes it coherent: admins live in the control and monitoring planes, never the data plane. That is how "an admin can revoke anyone and see who accessed what, yet cannot read a single clinical note" is true rather than marketing. There is no single god admin who both manages everyone and can read everything.

Minimum necessary, rendered

The front desk pane holds a time, a room, a name they already say out loud, and a confirmation. Four fields. Anything more is a design regression, and it is small enough to check by eye in a review.

Redaction is not the mechanism

The hatched block in the org-admin pane is what a UI-level control looks like — a thing that exists and is covered up. It appears once here to name the pattern we are not using. The other three panes are empty because there is no key, not because there is a filter.

The org does not own the client

A practice is a membership and addressing convenience. The client holds their keys, can see the current care team, and can prune it. Removing a clinician from the practice triggers revocation and key rotation.

Sending an invite does not grant anything. It creates an account that must then be granted, separately, by the person whose data it is.

What this account cannot do
  • Open a share, plan, note or score — no key exists for this role
  • Change anyone's role or add a clinician (org admin, with step-up)
  • See which self-checks a person has been given
  • Read the relationship audit log — that is the patient's and the org admin's

Disabled beats hidden, here

Elsewhere hiding is right. For staff roles, showing the greyed clinical section tells a scheduler the boundary exists and is deliberate, which is exactly the thing that stops someone asking a clinician to "just look it up for me".

grants held: 0

The rail footer states the account's key custody as a number, permanently. It is the cheapest possible audit: a front-desk account whose grant count is ever non-zero is a finding, visible without opening a log.

The one warm colour is a cancellation

Clay appears once, on a cancelled slot. No mood token is loaded on this route at all — which is also how you test it, by asserting the mood custom properties are absent from the computed style.

Withdrawing v2 removes it from their app and leaves a withdrawal in the lineage. Nothing is deleted from history — for them or for you.

Publishing needs step-up

Signing a plan into someone's app is a new, hard-to-unsee thing in their life. Re-auth once, here — and never again for reading what you already hold.

Withdrawal is deliberately one click and no re-auth. The safe direction is never the expensive one.

The item kinds are the only place the mood ramp bends

Goal is indigo (structure), but exercise and task borrow mood-4 and mood-3 as category tints. It is the one deliberate exception in the system, justified because these are the person's own content categories, not interface state — and it is worth arguing about in review.

Asymmetric friction, made visible

Publish carries step-up MFA; withdraw carries none. Putting both buttons in the same card is what makes the asymmetry a design statement instead of an implementation detail nobody notices.

Detail fields do the safety work

"If it makes you feel worse, stop" and "stays on your phone; I will never see it" are the kind of lines a plan needs. The composer should probably prompt for them rather than hope — a real open question for the clinician to answer.

What is this?

Anyone may publish Custom — it self-discloses and can never pose as clinical. Adapted and Validated are gated to credentialed roles at the access-control layer and require a resolving ledger reference. Your role does not carry them.

Nine checks, phrased as claims

"Band labels are descriptive, never a verdict" reads as a promise about the tool. "labels[] must match /^(?!.*\b(mild|moderate|severe)\b)/" reads as a form error. Same rule, and only one of them teaches the author what the product believes.

Two tiers are greyed, not absent

Adapted and Validated stay in the segmented control, disabled, with the reason stated. A clinician needs to know the tiers exist and that publishing one is a credential question, not a missing feature.

The gap at 16 is the good bug

It is exactly the mistake a careful person makes — three tidy ranges typed by hand — and it silently produces a "—" band months later. Catching it live, with the number named, is worth more than any amount of documentation.

Surface 4Data planeDelta, not a new app

A prescriber's account — three additions and one hard stop.

You asked whether a doctor's page would differ. Mostly no, and that is the right answer: the role catalog gives a psychiatrist the same reads as a clinician plus the ability to publish Validated and Adapted tools. The differences worth building are about co-treatment, not about medicine — because the moment this tool touches dosing it becomes a different product with a different regulatory life.

What changes

Three additions

Everything else is the clinician surface, unmodified.

Publish Adapted and Validated toolsrole-gated

The tier picker in the tool builder unlocks, and with it a required ledger reference that must resolve to a known anchor. A Validated tool shows its source citation instead of a warning.

Co-treating, visible on both sidescare_relationships

A care role of primary, co-treating, covering or supervising. The client sees the whole team and can prune it. A psychiatrist and a psychotherapist working the same case each hold their own grant.

Referral as a control-plane objectproposes, never moves a key

A referral proposes a relationship. It becomes real when the client consents, and consent is what issues the grant. One clinician never hands another their key.

Hard stop

What a prescriber account still is not

Naming this now is cheaper than removing it later.

Not a medication record

No drug list, no dose, no titration schedule, no interaction checking, no e-prescribing. Those are regulated clinical decision support and belong to an EHR that has been through that process.

Not a diagnosis field

There is no place to record one, deliberately. Every instrument in the catalog carries nonDiagnostic: true as a validated invariant, and a free-text plan is framed as guidance.

Not a clinical record of authority

It is the client's tool, and the client can revoke and prune. A practice that needs a record it controls needs an EHR; this sits alongside one, it does not replace it.

Not a crisis channel

Nothing here is monitored in real time. The safety plan is the person's own and is never a suggestion card; the crisis row hands off and does not dial.

Two of these are honest bad news
  • WebAuthn is a 501 stub. The stronger custody path the sign-in banner points at does not exist yet. Until it does, every clinician is on the lower-assurance TOTP path and the banner is the only thing telling them.
  • TOTP seeds are stored in cleartext in totp.secret_b64. That is structurally required for TOTP to work server-side, and it means a disk image is a factor-bypass. It is an argument for WebAuthn, not a bug to hide.
  • An admin reading this screen sees roles, factor kinds, credential ages and grant counts — never a grant's contents, never a client list, never a note.
Rank by kind, not by a percentage

"71% MFA coverage" would put WebAuthn and TOTP in the same bucket, which the product's own assurance banner refuses to do. Sorting the roster by factor strength makes the weakest accounts the first thing read, with no arithmetic in between.

grants: 0 does the work again

The same column that proves the front desk is safe proves the org admin is. It appears on the admin's own rail footer too — the person looking at this screen can see, at all times, that they hold none.

Bad news gets the amber panel

The WebAuthn stub and the cleartext TOTP seed are surfaced to the admin in the console rather than buried in COMPANION_SECURITY.md. An operator who cannot see the gap cannot make a decision about it.

Surface 6MonitoringSourced — check me

Compliance — what can actually be downloaded, and what cannot.

You asked whether there is a HIPAA tool out there that could be pulled in, kept updated, and run from the admin console. There are three separate things wearing that name, only one of which automates, and none of which makes anyone compliant. Splitting them is most of the answer.

Automatable

Continuous technical checks

Real software, self-hosted, pulls signature updates. This is the lane that can live in the console.

WazuhGPL-2.0 · self-hosted

Open-source SIEM/XDR whose default rules are tagged with HIPAA identifiers — a rule joins a requirement by putting hipaa_164.312.b in its group tag. Ships HIPAA dashboards; custom rules can be mapped the same way.

ruleset updates · automatic
OpenSCAP + SCAP Security Guidehost hardening

Machine-readable benchmark content for the host the container runs on. Answers "is the box configured the way we said", which is a large slice of the technical safeguards.

content updates · automatic
Trivy or Grype, against the pinned digestOSV feed

Scans the exact image the console already displays. The vulnerability database syncs on a schedule and works from a local mirror, which matters given this stack has no egress.

vuln DB · scheduled sync
Human, downloadable

The assessment itself

This is the closest thing to "a HIPAA tool you download and run" — and it produces a document, not a verdict.

HHS OCR / ASTP Security Risk Assessment Toolfree · v3.6.1

A downloadable Windows desktop app, with an Excel workbook alternative, published by the US government for small and medium practices. v3.6 added a reviewed-by confirmation that records approvals and dates for audit tracking, and moved its risk scale to NIST language.

updates · manual, by version
What it is not

It does not inspect your deployment, cannot see this server, and issues no certification. It walks an organisation through a structured self-assessment. The output is a risk analysis a practice must own, revisit, and act on — which is exactly what the Security Rule asks for.

How the console helps

By exporting evidence the assessment asks for and cannot otherwise get: session expiry settings, factor coverage per account, audit retention, encryption posture, the image digest, and the dates of the last review. Fill-in-the-blanks, not a score.

Machine-readable law

Control catalogs you can diff

The format that lets a checklist be pulled and re-pulled when the rule changes.

NIST SP 800-66r2final · free

NIST's guide to implementing the HIPAA Security Rule. Appendix D catalogs each standard and implementation specification and maps it to Cybersecurity Framework subcategories and SP 800-53r5 controls — the mapping that turns a legal clause into something testable.

revision · rare, versioned
NIST CPRT + OSCALmachine-readable

The mappings are published through NIST's reference tool, and OSCAL is the standard format for representing control catalogs as data. Storing the readiness checklist in OSCAL rather than a markdown list is what makes "the rule changed, what moved?" a diff instead of a re-read.

catalog fetch · scriptable
The rule is still moving

The proposed Security Rule overhaul was published 6 January 2025 and is not final. OMB's Unified Agenda now targets July 2027 for final action, pushed back from spring 2026, and over 4,000 comments were filed. The current Security Rule remains in force.

watch · RIN 0945-AA22

The sentence that has to survive contact with a real practice

Software is HIPAA-ready; a deployment plus an organisation is what is compliant. That line is already in COMPANION_ACCESS_CONTROL.md, and every screen in this deck has to keep it true. A console can show that encryption is on, that sessions expire, that the audit chain verifies, that two accounts have no second factor. It cannot show that a practice has written policies, trained its workforce, signed business associate agreements, or has a breach-notification procedure — and those are not edge cases, they are most of the Security Rule.

So the honest console does two things and refuses a third. It runs the technical checks continuously and shows them plainly. It exports evidence into the format a human assessment wants. And it never renders a compliance score, a percentage, or a green shield — because the one thing worse than no compliance tooling is a dashboard that tells a small practice it is compliant when it is not.

Your repo already names the gate, and it is stricter than anything above: an external HIPAA Security-Rule assessment and an independent crypto/RBAC audit before any real patient. The proposed rule's headline requirements — MFA everywhere, encryption at rest, asset inventory, annual audits — are mostly things this design already intends, so building toward it is a cheap bet even though it may not land until 2027.

Self-auditing is the strongest lane

You are right that it matters most. The hash-chained log, the grant-count invariants, the "front desk holds zero grants" assertion — those are checks the system can run against itself continuously, and unlike a downloaded questionnaire they fail loudly when someone breaks them.

Egress is a design constraint here

The compose stack deliberately has no route out. Every feed above has to work from a local mirror or a scheduled, operator-initiated sync — which is a feature, since a compliance agent that phones home is a new data-exfiltration path in a product built to have none.

Beware the vendor lane

There is a large market of "HIPAA compliance platforms" that are SaaS, require an account, and want your evidence in their cloud. For a self-hosted, zero-knowledge product that is the wrong shape — it would put a third party closer to the data than the sysadmin is.

Before anyone forwards this

Status of everything above.

The model is written. The screens are not.

  • The role catalog, three planes, consent model, friction budget and behavioural guard are quoted from docs/COMPANION_ACCESS_CONTROL.md. They are a specification, not shipped code — the running system is still one owner ↔ one pinned therapist, roles OWNER / THERAPIST only.
  • There is no org, no practice, no front desk account, no org admin and no prescriber role in the build today. All seven surfaces here are proposals.
  • The tool builder's gate is real — builder.ts and validate.ts exist and run those checks. The screen around them does not.
  • The plan builder exists as GamePlanAuthor.svelte with lineage, sealing and step-up. The composition, ordering and lineage panel shown here are new.
  • WebAuthn is a 501 stub and TOTP seeds are stored in cleartext. Both are stated in the repo and reproduced faithfully in the admin render, including on a screen an operator would actually read.
  • The compliance lane names real, checkable software and documents, linked below. Nothing there has been integrated, evaluated in this codebase, or tested — treat it as a shortlist to verify, not a recommendation.
  • I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. The regulatory dates are sourced but timelines move; verify against the Federal Register before making a decision on them.
  • Every account name, timestamp, hash and count on these screens is invented.
