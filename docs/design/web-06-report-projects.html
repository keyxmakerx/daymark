What changed since the last render, and why
  • True A4 scale. The previous render was web-sized, so 14 px type sat in a page that represents 595 pt. It looked enormous and told you nothing about legibility.
  • The QR is back. Dropping it was a regression — QrEncoder already ships, and the verification payload is a real feature no redesign should have quietly removed.
  • The journal is back, as its own side. A whole page someone consciously switches on is a very different act from a sentence lifted into a chart. It resolves the excerpt problem instead of dodging it.
  • Check-in notes were never the issue. The one-line note attached to a check-in is part of the check-in and always was — it is on side 2.
  • Streaks dropped. They are in ReportData today. A streak measures adherence to the app, not the person, and it is the one number here that could make a bad fortnight look like a failure.

NamingMy pick marked

Three for the clinician platform.

Daymark is a nautical term — an unlit marker you navigate by in daylight. These stay in that world without being cute.

Casement

a window hinged to open outward

Literally what it is: a window the client opened from their side and can close. Already the sign-in copy. Implies a limited view rather than a full record — the honest promise.

Bearing

a direction taken from a fixed point

What a clinician does between sessions. Sits naturally with Daymark, carries the second meaning of composure. More clinical in feel, which may be a plus.

Cairn

stones stacked to mark a path

Built by hand by people who came through before, marking a route without prescribing it. Warmest, weakest fit for a data tool.
