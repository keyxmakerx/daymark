Daymark Companion / clinician web

Cool chrome,
warm content.

Six screens for the clinician side of Daymark, plus the server admin console. The current build dresses everything as warm paper, which makes a working tool read like a personal essay. Termix and Zerobyte dress everything as chrome, which is why they feel sharp — and why they feel edgy. The merge is a rule, not a mood board: chrome gets the cool, dense, monospaced discipline; a person's data keeps the paper. Neither crosses into the other.

The rule

One split decides every colour, every typeface, every border radius.

Ask of any pixel: is this the person, or is this the machinery? Everything follows. It also answers the two complaints at once — the interface gains the second and third colour dimensions it was missing, and it gains them without turning a mental-health tool into a terminal.

Content layer — the person

Their answers, their bands, their trend, the words they read. Unhurried, generous, set in serif on paper. This is where the mood scale lives and the only place it lives.

  • Paper sheets, 10px radius, soft elevation
  • Serif display, 1.6 line-height, room to breathe
  • Mood 1–5 encodes data, never interface state
  • Sentence case, plain language, no clinical framing

Chrome layer — the machinery

Navigation, tables, timestamps, key fingerprints, capability ids, statuses, the admin console. Cool grey-blue, tight, monospaced, tabular. Borrowed straight from Termix and Zerobyte — and confined here so it never touches the content.

  • chrome ground · 4–5px radius · 1px hairlines
  • mono + tabular-nums for every number
  • indigo #3f5f7f = structure only, never data
  • clay #a8574a = the one alarm hue in the system
SystemNew dimensions

Three colour dimensions where there was one.

Today the mood ramp does double duty: it colours a person's score and every warning, tab, and banner in the app. That is the "only one colour" feeling. Splitting them gives the mood ramp back to the data and hands the interface its own vocabulary — a structural accent, a single alarm hue, and a status ramp that carries most of its meaning in form rather than hue.

Why "done" is ink, not green

In a product whose trust strip is forbidden from ever going green, a green checkmark would be a claim. Completion is a fact, not a verdict — so it is set in solid ink, and the only chroma in the whole status ramp belongs to things that need a human.

Fonts are declared but not shipped

app.css names Fraunces and Inter, and web/fonts/ contains only a README. Every screen you are looking at — and every screen a clinician sees today — is running the fallback stack. Vendoring the two subset woff2 files is a one-commit fix.

The dark console does not flip

The admin surface stays dark in light mode too. It is a different room, entered deliberately, and holding it fixed is what stops the console's severity colours from having to work on two grounds.

Daymark Companion

A window your client opened for you.

Daymark lives on your client's phone. This page shows only the slice they chose to hand over, on a server they or someone they trust runs. Nothing here is a diagnosis, a screen, or a clinical record.

What you get
  • Scores and descriptive bands from self-checks they completed
  • The ability to suggest self-checks, tasks, goals and reminders
  • A written game plan you author, that they can accept or ignore
  • Dates: what is due, what arrived, what went unanswered
What you never get
  • Raw journal entries, notes, or anything they wrote
  • Location, contacts, or anything from the rest of their phone
  • The power to make anything happen — every item needs their acceptance
  • Access that outlives their revocation, which is instant and unilateral

Unlock this browser session

Lower-assurance path (TOTP). Your reading key is unwrapped in this browser under a passphrase. Because the page is served by the server it talks to, a tampered page could capture your passphrase or keys — a convenience path, not a zero-knowledge guarantee. Keys are held in memory only and wiped when you log out or go idle.

The banner keeps its exact words

That paragraph is not design copy — it is fixed, non-server-supplied text from LowerAssuranceBanner.svelte. The redesign changes where it sits and how it is weighted, never what it says.

The digest is a control, not a footnote

The banner tells the reader to verify the image digest and then, today, gives them nothing to verify against. Putting the pinned digest on the sign-in page turns an instruction into an action.

Warm left, cool right

The explanation is content, so it is paper and serif. The credential form is machinery, so it is chrome. The seam down the middle is the whole system stated once, before a clinician has learned any of it.

Bands are descriptive summaries of a person's own answers over time — a within-person trend, never a screen, a cut-off, or a diagnosis. "Harder stretch" is not a severity level.

Names belong to the clinician, not the server

The server stores relationships as an opaque relRef. The roster should render a label the clinician sets in their own browser, with the ref shown beneath it — so the box on the shelf never becomes the place client identities live.

Six rows, one colour of alarm

Five of six rows are quiet on purpose. If "Declined" and "Delivered" both shouted, the one that matters would not. Clay appears three times on this screen and each one is a genuine exception.

"Not shared yet" is a real state

A relationship can exist with nothing shared through it. Rendering that as an empty band rather than a zero keeps the table from implying a low score where there is simply no data.

Shape carries the kind, colour carries the state

Due is an indigo bar, shared is a filled mood square, a session is an outline, a lapse is clay. Four event types stay distinguishable in a 4×11px mark, and the whole thing still reads if you cannot separate red from green.

The calendar shows that, never what

A shared-data mark says a bundle arrived and which band it carried. It does not open an entry, because there are no entries here to open — the constraint is upstream of the design.

Hover lifts, click commits

Cells rise 1px and take elevation on hover; selection is a 2px indigo inset that survives month changes. Marks bloom in on a 24ms stagger when a month renders — enough to read as arrival, short enough to never be waited on.

The edge of this screen
  • Scores and descriptive bands, exactly as the catalog computes them
  • Which tool, which version, and what tier it claims
  • When a bundle was shared and when you opened it — both logged for them
  • Item-level answers, free-text notes, journal entries

Every catalog tool today is Custom — self-authored original items, deliberately not the ASRS, WHO-5, or any licensed scale. There is no Validated tier in this build, and the badge is there so you never have to guess.

The y-axis is bands, not numbers

Plotting a raw sum invites a clinician to read a cut-off that does not exist. Labelling the axis with the same descriptive bands the person sees on their phone keeps both sides of the relationship looking at the same object.

Provenance appears four times

Once per row and once in the header, because a clinician who scrolls past the disclaimer once should still meet it at the point of reading a number. Custom ✎ is the amber badge — the only place amber appears outside the console.

Withdrawn capabilities render as absent

The greyed rows are what this person has not granted. If the signed grant fails to verify against the pinned owner key, none of this screen renders at all — the current build already refuses, and the redesign keeps that refusal loud.

What this console must never show
  • Blob contents, keys, TOTP seeds or codes, or which individual record was read. The audit taxonomy names events, never content — and this console reads the same table.
  • A clinician's relationship history to an admin who is not the owner. The audit log is owner-readable. Admin sees counts and integrity, not another person's trail.
  • Turning on DAYMARK_ACCESS_LOG_SOURCE_IP converts this log into a record of where people were. Off by default, and the console should say so where you flip it.
  • Raising DAYMARK_ACCESS_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS past 90 raises exactly how much a seized disk reveals. Retention is a privacy control wearing an ops label.
  • DAYMARK_TRUSTED_PROXIES unset means every lockout keys on the proxy, so one bucket serves everybody — the 1 distinct bucket above is that bug, visible.
  • Opening this console is itself an event. If admin access is not logged somewhere the admin cannot edit, the log's promise is only as good as the admin.
No green in the console either

Severity runs faint → amber → clay, and "healthy" is simply the absence of colour. A green "all clear" would be the same overclaim the trust strip is forbidden from making, one layer down.

"1 distinct bucket" is a finding, not a stat

With DAYMARK_TRUSTED_PROXIES unset, every client shares one rate-limit bucket, so eight bad tokens from one attacker lock out everybody. Today that misconfiguration fails silently. Here it is a red number next to the config that caused it.

The chain caveat is not small print

It gets a full paragraph directly under the verdict, at body size, because "418 / 418 verified" is precisely the phrase that would otherwise be read as "nothing was hidden from me". Internal consistency is not completeness.

Motion

Six movements, and a rule about when they run.

Unhurried means slow enough to read as arrival and short enough that nobody waits on it: 120–160ms for anything under a pointer, 400–700ms for anything entering the page, and a single easing curve throughout. Nothing loops, nothing pulses, nothing bounces. Every one of these collapses to zero under prefers-reduced-motion, including the console's live tail, which stops appending rather than appending invisibly.

What these renders are not.

Nothing here is built.

  • Four of these six screens do not exist in any form. There is no calendar, no Today, no client record, and no admin console anywhere in companion/web/src.
  • The two "redesigned" screens exist as a bare login and a tab strip. Everything shown around them is proposal.
  • The admin console reads from tables and config keys that are real, but the endpoint, the credential separating admin from owner, and the integrity check are all unbuilt.
  • Band labels, trend arrows and the 12-week ribbon assume a summarisation layer that has not been written or reviewed.
  • Fraunces and Inter are named in app.css and absent from web/fonts/. These renders use the fallback stack, exactly like the live build.
  • Every score, name, timestamp, digest and hash on these screens is invented. The tool titles, capability ids, audit actions and fixed banner copy are not — those are quoted from the repository.
